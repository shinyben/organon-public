I noticed a number of similarities between Murakami's *Kafka* and Salinger's *Catcher* when reading. My best search gave one academic paper ([Cruz 2018, IJELLH](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/341026970_An_Intertextual_Study_of_Haruki_Murakami's_Kafka_on_the_Shore)) noting similarities between characters (Holden and Kafka, Phoebe and Sakura, Mr. Antolini and Oshima) as well as the novels both dealing with  young men wandering around in the world. There also seemed to be a number of people referencing the books feeling similar on Goodreads and whatnot, but no listing of similarities like this. 

Suitcases as wealth/class tell

- "it's really hard to be roommates with people if your suitcases are much better than theirs ... At least his suitcases were as good as mine."
- "a nothing kind of suitcase she must've picked up at some discount place"

Hotel window with nothing but a wall to look at

- "They gave me this very crumby room, with nothing to look out of the window at except the other side of the hotel."
- "The view out the window is of the wall of the building next door."

Prostitute scene, attempt at deeper connection

- "do you mind very much if we don't do it? Do you mind very much? ... I felt more depressed than sexy."
- "I recommend Hegel. He's sort of out of date, but definitely an oldie but goodie."

The word "phony" (repeated often in *Catcher*) applied to identity-associated things

- "I should've given her a phony name, but I didn't think of it."
- "a phony address and age ... nobody yells out, _Hey, we can see right through your ruse._ ... Of course I gave a phony home phone number"

Museum/library grounding the wandering

- "The best thing, though, in that museum was that everything always stayed right where it was. Nobody'd move. ... The only thing that would be diﬀerent would be you. Not that you’d be so much older or anything. It wouldn’t be that, exactly. You’d just be diﬀerent, that’s all."
- "The library was like a second home. Or maybe more like a real home."

A plan to run away 

- "I really decided to go out West and all."
- "On my fifteenth birthday I'll run away from home, journey to a far-off town, and live in a corner of a small library."
